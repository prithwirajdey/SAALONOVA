2 · Enter data

Rows are treatment combinations (last factor varying fastest); columns are replications within each year. Copy the numeric block from Excel and paste into the first cell; a leading label column is detected and skipped. Enter / ↑ ↓ move between rows.

Columns: Year, Rep, Factor1[, Factor2, Factor3], Value, one row per plot (comma, tab or semicolon separated; header optional). The number of factor columns must suit the chosen design (CRD/RBD: 1–3, split and strip plot: 2, split-split plot: 3). Levels are read in order of first appearance and the dimensions are set automatically.

Method and formulae

Year-wise ANOVA. CRD: treatments (and their factorial components), Error = t(r−1). RBD: Replication, treatments, Error = (r−1)(t−1). Split plot: Replication, A, Error(a) = R×A, B, A×B, Error(b). Strip plot: Replication, A, Error(a) = R×A, B, Error(b) = R×B, A×B, Error(c) = R×A×B. Split-split plot: Replication, A, Error(a), B, A×B, Error(b) = R×B + R×A×B, C, A×C, B×C, A×B×C, Error(c). Each effect is tested against the error of its own stratum; sums of squares follow the standard balanced-data formulae (Gomez & Gomez, 1984; Panse & Sukhatme).

Homogeneity. Before pooling, the error mean squares of each stratum are compared across years with Bartlett's χ² test (df = y−1); Fmax = largest/smallest MS is also given. Pool only when errors are homogeneous; otherwise interpret year-wise results.

Pooled ANOVA (years fixed; Gomez & Gomez, 1984, ch. 8). Years are tested against Replications within years (against pooled error in CRD). Every treatment term T and its interaction Y×T are tested against the pooled error of T's stratum; pooled errors are the sums of the yearly error SS and df.

Standard errors. For every comparison the variance of a difference is built from the stratum variance components, so the familiar formulae are reproduced exactly: e.g. split plot, A at same or different B: SEd = √{2[(b−1)Eb + Ea]/rb}; strip plot, A at same B: √{2[(b−1)Ec + Ea]/rb}; split-split plot, B at same A: √(2Eb/rc). SEm(±) = SEd/√2 and CD = SEd × t. Comparisons that involve two error terms use the weighted t′ of Cochran and Cox; in pooled tables r is replaced by ry.

Letter groupings. Means are ranked, the highest receives “a”, and means sharing a letter do not differ significantly. LSD uses CD; DMRT uses the studentised range q(p, df, αp) with αp = 1 − (1 − α)p−1 for means p ranks apart; Tukey HSD uses q(k, df, α). The studentised range distribution is computed by numerical integration; comparisons involving two error terms use Satterthwaite's df. Letters are assigned with the insert-and-absorb algorithm (Piepho, 2004), so they remain correct when different pairs of cells in an interaction table carry different standard errors.

Dispersion. SD of replicates is the SD of the replicate-wise means of that cell (n = r for a year, n = ry for pooled); SD of all plots uses every plot contributing to the mean. CV = √E/GM × 100 for each error stratum.
